NVIDIA generated $115.844 billion in its fiscal third quarter ended October 25, 2026, but the most revealing split is inside its $107.610 billion Data Center business. Hyperscale customers accounted for $60.136 billion, while AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise contributed $47.474 billion, according to a November 17 company disclosure. Another $8.234 billion came from Edge Computing. SmartChart #29 maps the entire reported market-platform hierarchy into a proportional treemap.

The findings complement earlier coverage of NVIDIA's 110% year-over-year Data Center increase without duplicating the earnings headline. The new question is where the revenue was recognized: among the largest cloud customers, a combined group of AI cloud providers and other businesses, or computing platforms at the edge.

SmartChart #29: Where $115.844 billion came from

Hyperscale represents 51.91% of NVIDIA's total quarterly revenue. AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise accounts for 40.98%, and Edge Computing represents 7.11%. The first two branches add to $107.610 billion of Data Center revenue, or 92.89% of the company total. All three figures sum exactly to $115.844 billion; no separate reportable financial segment is added a second time.

The most consequential comparison is growth outside the Hyperscale category. AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise revenue jumped from $16.075 billion in the year-earlier quarter to $47.474 billion, an increase of approximately 195.3%. Hyperscale rose from $35.140 billion to $60.136 billion, a 71.1% gain. Edge Computing increased 42.2% to $8.234 billion. Historical comparisons use NVIDIA's recast market-platform series, reflecting a prior customer reclassification.

The categories matter more than the headline

NVIDIA's market-platform table differs from its accounting reportable segments, Compute and Networking and Graphics. Neither breakdown should be combined with the other because doing so would count overlapping revenue twice. AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise also aggregates several types of buyers; the disclosure does not separate an exact neocloud or industrial dollar amount.

Customer concentration remains an important limitation. NVIDIA's supplemental filing says three direct customers accounted for 14%, 11% and 11% of total revenue. Their identities and exact relationships to the market-platform categories cannot be derived from the treemap. Large commitments to AI infrastructure also depend on financing, electricity, interconnects, cooling and delivery schedules.

Related developments include Arista's optical networking systems, TSMC's Arizona capacity plan and industrial electricity price differences. None provides a direct allocation of NVIDIA revenue, but each helps explain constraints around large-scale deployments.
